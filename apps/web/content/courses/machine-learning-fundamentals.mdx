## What you will learn

A proper understanding of classical ML — not just how to call `model.fit()` but why each algorithm works, what its assumptions are, and when it will fail.

### Supervised learning
- Linear and logistic regression — geometry and probability interpretations
- Decision trees and random forests — information gain, bagging, feature importance
- Support Vector Machines — the kernel trick, margin maximisation
- Gradient boosting: XGBoost, LightGBM — why they win tabular competitions
- Evaluation: confusion matrices, ROC/AUC, precision/recall trade-offs

### Unsupervised learning
- K-means clustering — convergence, limitations, choosing K
- Hierarchical clustering
- Gaussian Mixture Models
- Principal Component Analysis — geometric and algebraic view
- Autoencoders as learnable dimensionality reduction

### Regularisation and generalisation
- Overfitting and underfitting — the bias-variance trade-off
- L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) regularisation
- Dropout and early stopping
- Cross-validation strategies

### Reinforcement learning (introduction)
- Markov Decision Processes
- Q-learning and policy gradients
- Where RL shows up in modern AI (RLHF for LLMs)

## Course outline

1. The ML problem: fitting, generalising, failing
2. Linear regression — least squares to gradient descent
3. Classification: logistic regression and decision boundaries
4. Tree-based models: from decision trees to gradient boosting
5. Support Vector Machines
6. Clustering: K-means, hierarchical, GMMs
7. Dimensionality reduction: PCA and autoencoders
8. The bias-variance trade-off and regularisation
9. Evaluation metrics that actually matter
10. Introduction to reinforcement learning
